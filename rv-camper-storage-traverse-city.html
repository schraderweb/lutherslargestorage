The Importance of Proper RV & Camper Storage

Owning an RV or camper is an investment in adventure, comfort, and freedom. However, with this investment comes the need for proper maintenance and care, especially during the off-season or when it’s not in use. Storing your RV or camper at home can often pose challenges, from limited space in the driveway to neighborhood restrictions on oversized vehicle parking.

In Traverse City, where harsh winters can take a toll on your vehicle, leaving your RV or camper outside unprotected can lead to significant wear and tear. Exposure to snow, rain, UV rays, and fluctuating temperatures can cause issues such as:

  1. Exterior Damage: Prolonged exposure to the elements can result in fading paint, cracked seals, and rusting. An uncovered RV is particularly susceptible to water damage, which can affect both the exterior and interior over time.

  2. Tire Wear: Even when parked, tires can degrade if not properly stored. Freezing and thawing cycles in Traverse City winters can lead to cracks in your tires and reduce their lifespan.

  3. Mechanical Issues: Moisture can lead to rusting in mechanical parts, while extreme temperatures can affect batteries, engines, and other essential systems.

  4. Security Risks: Leaving your RV or camper parked in an open lot or your driveway increases the risk of theft or vandalism. Securing your vehicle in a dedicated storage facility offers greater peace of mind.

How to Prepare Your RV or Camper for Storage

Before storing your RV or camper, there are a few essential steps you should take to ensure it remains in top condition while in storage:

  1. Clean the Exterior and Interior: Give your RV a thorough wash to remove any dirt, debris, or road salt that could cause corrosion. Inside, clean and disinfect surfaces, and make sure all food items are removed to avoid attracting pests.

  2. Empty Tanks and Lines: Be sure to drain all water from the plumbing system, including the water heater and holding tanks. Add antifreeze if you’re storing your RV during winter months to prevent pipes from freezing.

  3. Check Tires: Inflate your tires to the manufacturer’s recommended pressure, and if possible, move the RV slightly every few months to prevent flat spots from forming.

  4. Disconnect the Battery: If your RV will be in storage for an extended period, disconnect the battery to prevent it from draining. You may also want to consider removing it and storing it separately in a warm, dry place.

  5. Cover the RV or Camper: Even if your RV is stored indoors, a cover can provide an extra layer of protection against dust and potential scratches.
